## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines how UrbanStitch staff should handle merchandise reported as defective.

It applies to suspected manufacturing defects and product-quality failures.

It does not apply to normal wear, accidental damage, misuse, improper washing, or unauthorized alteration.

---

## 2. Defect Assessment Window

Customers may report a suspected manufacturing defect within **30 calendar days** from the purchase date for in-store purchases or the confirmed delivery date for online purchases.

Cases reported after 30 days are not automatically eligible and should be escalated for review if the customer claims the issue is manufacturing-related.

---

## 3. Examples of Possible Manufacturing Defects

Possible manufacturing defects may include:

- broken or failed zipper under normal use
- failed stitching or seams
- detached buttons, snaps, or hardware
- abnormal fabric tearing without customer misuse
- incorrect assembly
- material defects
- product construction failure
- other quality issues caused by production

The presence of damage does not automatically mean the item has a manufacturing defect.

Staff must inspect or document the reported issue before confirming eligibility.

---

## 4. Cases Not Considered Manufacturing Defects

The following are normally not considered manufacturing defects:

- accidental customer damage
- damage caused by improper washing or drying
- damage caused by misuse
- unauthorized alterations
- normal wear and tear
- staining caused after purchase
- damage caused by improper storage
- loss of parts caused by customer handling

If staff cannot determine whether the damage is customer-caused or manufacturing-related, the case must be escalated.

---

## 5. Required Verification

Before confirming a defective merchandise resolution, staff should verify:

- valid proof of purchase
- purchase or delivery date
- reported defect
- physical or photographic evidence of the issue
- whether the damage appears manufacturing-related
- whether the request falls within the 30-day defect assessment window

Staff should record enough information to support the decision.

---

## 6. Defect Inspection

For in-store cases, staff should inspect the item whenever practical.

For online cases, staff may request clear photos or other evidence showing the defect.

The inspection should determine whether the issue is:

UNCLEAR / REQUIRES REVIEW
CUSTOMER-CAUSED OR NON-DEFECT DAMAGE

A customer statement alone should not be treated as confirmation of a manufacturing defect.

7. Resolution for Confirmed Manufacturing Defects

When a manufacturing defect is confirmed, the item may qualify for an appropriate resolution.

Possible resolutions may include:

replacement with the same item
exchange for an equivalent item
store credit
refund, when permitted by applicable policy

The exact resolution depends on stock availability, applicable policies, and any required approval.

Refund authorization must follow:

DOC-004 — Refund Approval Matrix

8. Final-Sale Merchandise

A confirmed manufacturing defect may qualify for an exception to normal final-sale restrictions.

Staff should also review:

DOC-003 — Final Sale & Promotion Policy

Final-sale status does not automatically prevent a resolution when a manufacturing defect is confirmed.

However, the defect must still be verified.

9. Standard Return Requirements

Defective merchandise should not be rejected only because it fails one of the normal return conditions in:

DOC-001 — Returns & Exchanges Policy

For example, a defective item may already have been worn or have detached tags before the defect became apparent.

When a manufacturing defect is confirmed, this SOP takes priority over normal return eligibility requirements where the two conflict.

10. Refund Approval Requirements

A confirmed defect does not automatically authorize a refund.

If a refund is selected as the resolution, staff must check the applicable approval threshold in:

DOC-004 — Refund Approval Matrix

Example:

Manufacturing defect confirmed
→ item may qualify for resolution

Refund requested
→ check refund approval threshold

Required approval missing
→ do not process refund
11. Cases Requiring Escalation

Staff should escalate when:

the cause of damage is unclear
available evidence is insufficient
the case is outside the 30-day assessment window
the customer disputes the staff assessment
multiple policies appear to conflict
the requested resolution falls outside documented options
management judgment is required
another policy explicitly requires escalation

Use:

DOC-007 — Customer Service Escalation SOP

12. Documentation Requirements

Staff should record:

customer-reported issue
item involved
purchase or delivery date
defect evidence
inspection result
applicable policy or SOP
proposed resolution
approval requirement, if any
escalation reason, if applicable

Documentation should clearly distinguish between:

customer claim
staff observation
confirmed defect
final resolution
