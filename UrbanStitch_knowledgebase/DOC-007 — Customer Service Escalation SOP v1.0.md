**Department:** Customer Service / Retail Operations  
**Audience:** Customer Service Staff, Store Staff, Store Managers, Retail Operations Managers

---

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines when UrbanStitch staff should escalate a customer case instead of making a final decision at frontline level.

Escalation should be used when:

- available policies do not clearly resolve the case;
- important evidence is missing;
- multiple active policies appear to conflict;
- management approval is required;
- the customer requests an exception outside documented rules; or
- the case involves elevated legal, financial, security, or reputational risk.

Escalation is a valid operational outcome and should not be treated as a failure.

---

## 2. General Escalation Principle

Frontline staff should resolve routine cases using the applicable UrbanStitch policy or SOP whenever the documented rules are clear.

Staff should escalate when the available information is not enough to support a safe and documented decision.

Staff should not guess, invent exceptions, or bypass required approval simply to close a case faster.

---

## 3. Standard Escalation Levels

UrbanStitch uses the following escalation levels.

### Level 1 — Store Manager or Customer Service Supervisor

Use Level 1 escalation when:

- a frontline employee cannot confidently apply the relevant policy;
- a customer disputes a normal policy decision;
- evidence is incomplete or unclear;
- a refund requires Store Manager approval;
- staff need management review before continuing;
- the case involves a non-standard but relatively low-risk exception.

---

### Level 2 — Retail Operations Manager

Use Level 2 escalation when:

- a refund exceeds the Store Manager approval threshold;
- multiple active policies appear to conflict;
- the requested resolution falls outside standard documented options;
- the case has significant financial impact;
- the case may affect multiple stores or customers;
- a Store Manager cannot resolve the issue safely.

---

### Level 3 — Specialized Management or Internal Team

Use Level 3 escalation when the case involves:

- suspected fraud;
- account compromise;
- serious security concern;
- legal threat;
- regulatory complaint;
- major reputational risk;
- significant operational incident;
- another area requiring specialized internal review.

Frontline staff should not attempt to make independent legal, security, or compliance decisions.

---

## 4. Escalation for Policy Conflicts

If two or more active policies appear to give conflicting instructions, staff should not choose one arbitrarily.

Staff should:

1. Identify the policies involved.
2. Confirm that each policy is active and applicable.
3. Identify the specific conflicting rules.
4. Record the customer or operational context.
5. Escalate to the appropriate manager for resolution.

If one document is archived and another is active, the active document normally takes priority.

Archived documents should not override current policy.

---

## 5. Escalation for Insufficient Evidence

A case should be escalated when important evidence is missing and the missing information could materially change the outcome.

Examples include:

- proof of purchase cannot be verified;
- shipment status is contradictory;
- defect cause cannot be determined;
- promotion terms are unavailable;
- refund amount is unclear;
- customer identity or transaction ownership cannot be verified;
- approval status cannot be confirmed.

Staff should document what information is missing.

---

## 6. Escalation for Refund Approval

Refund approval must follow:

**DOC-004 — Refund Approval Matrix**

Typical escalation paths are:

**Refund of ₱10,000 or below**  
→ Normal authorized retail process, unless another issue requires escalation.

**Refund from ₱10,001 to ₱25,000**  
→ Store Manager approval required.

**Refund above ₱25,000**  
→ Retail Operations Manager approval required.

Staff should not reduce, split, or modify a refund amount to avoid escalation.

---

## 7. Escalation for Defective Merchandise

Use escalation when:

- it is unclear whether damage is manufacturing-related;
- the reported defect is outside the normal assessment window;
- customer evidence and staff observations conflict;
- the requested resolution is not documented;
- the customer disputes the defect assessment.

Use:

**DOC-002 — Defective Merchandise SOP**

before escalating whenever applicable.

---

## 8. Escalation for Final-Sale and Promotional Cases

Use escalation when:

- final-sale designation cannot be verified;
- promotion terms are missing or unclear;
- the customer claims an undocumented exception;
- multiple active promotional rules appear to conflict;
- staff cannot determine whether the normal final-sale restriction applies.

Use:

**DOC-003 — Final Sale & Promotion Policy**

before escalating whenever applicable.

---

## 9. Escalation for Shipping and Delivery Claims

Use escalation when:

- tracking data is contradictory;
- a package cannot be confidently classified as lost;
- delivery evidence is disputed;
- staff cannot distinguish shipping damage from manufacturing defect;
- a customer requests a resolution outside documented shipping procedures;
- the claim involves unusually high-value merchandise.

Use:

**DOC-005 — Shipping & Delivery Claims SOP**

before escalating whenever applicable.

---

## 10. Escalation for Online Order Cancellations

Use escalation when:

- fulfillment status cannot be verified;
- the order system provides conflicting information;
- suspected fraud or unauthorized activity is involved;
- the customer requests cancellation after normal cancellation is no longer available;
- cancellation and refund requirements appear to conflict.

Use:

**DOC-006 — Online Order Cancellation Policy**

before escalating whenever applicable.

---

## 11. Legal Threats and Formal Complaints

If a customer threatens legal action, claims regulatory violations, or states that they will file a formal complaint with a government or regulatory body, frontline staff should avoid making legal conclusions.

The case should be escalated to the appropriate manager or specialized internal team.

Staff may continue to document factual information but should not:

- admit legal liability;
- make unsupported legal promises;
- invent compensation;
- override policy without authorization.

---

## 12. Security and Fraud Concerns

Cases involving suspected:

- stolen accounts
- unauthorized purchases
- payment fraud
- identity misuse
- compromised employee access
- suspicious customer activity

should be escalated through the appropriate security or management process.

Staff should not rely solely on customer statements to establish identity or authority.

---

## 13. Information Required for Escalation

When escalating a case, staff should include enough context so the next reviewer does not have to restart the investigation.

The escalation record should include:

- case or request identifier, if available;
- customer-reported issue;
- relevant order or transaction reference;
- affected product;
- verified facts;
- missing or disputed information;
- policies or SOPs reviewed;
- requested customer resolution;
- current recommendation, if any;
- reason for escalation;
- approval requirement, if applicable.

---

## 14. Distinguishing Facts From Claims

Escalation notes should distinguish between:

**Customer Claim**  
What the customer says happened.

**Verified Fact**  
Information confirmed through trusted records or staff review.

**Policy Requirement**  
What UrbanStitch documentation requires.

**Staff Recommendation**  
What staff believe should happen next.

These categories should not be mixed together.

---

## 15. Escalation Does Not Equal Approval

Sending a case to a manager does not mean the requested resolution has been approved.

For example:

**Escalated refund request**  
does not mean  
**Approved refund**

The case must remain pending until the required decision or approval is actually recorded.

---

## 16. Escalation Priority

Cases should generally be prioritized based on impact and risk.

### Standard Priority

Use for routine policy clarification or non-urgent exceptions.

### High Priority

Use when the case involves:

- high-value refund;
- unresolved delivery failure;
- repeated customer impact;
- significant operational disruption;
- senior management review.

### Critical Priority

Use when the case involves:

- active security risk;
- suspected fraud with ongoing exposure;
- serious legal or regulatory concern;
- major reputational incident;
- safety-related issue.

Priority should reflect actual risk, not customer pressure alone.

---

## 17. Cases That Should Not Be Escalated Automatically

Not every unhappy customer requires management escalation.

Frontline staff should normally continue handling the case when:

- the applicable policy is clear;
- required evidence is available;
- the resolution falls within normal authority;
- no approval threshold is triggered;
- no unusual security, legal, or operational risk exists.

Escalation should be purposeful.

---

## 18. Resolution After Escalation

Once a manager or authorized team makes a decision, staff should record:

- final decision;
- person or role making the decision;
- approved resolution;
- approval amount, if applicable;
- any conditions attached to the decision;
- date of decision;
- next required action.

If the approved resolution differs materially from the original request, the final record should reflect the actual approved outcome.
